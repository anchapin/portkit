### [2607.06184] What Resolve Rate Hides: Trajectory Structure Diagnostics for Coding Agents (TraceProbe)
- **arXiv:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.06184
- **Submitted:** July 2026 · cs.SE
- **Relevance:** 80% — multi_agent_qa
- **GitHub Issue:** [#1876](https://github.com/anchapin/portkit/issues/1876) — *Adopt TraceProbe-Style Trajectory Diagnostics for the Translation Pipeline*

**Summary:** Coding agents are almost universally ranked by resolve rate alone, which hides *why* a run failed or why an accepted run wasted steps/time/tokens. TraceProbe normalizes raw execution trajectories into a canonical nine-type action taxonomy, then applies two rule-based modules: Insight (single-trajectory anti-patterns like search loops and verification skips) and Converge (aligns pairs of runs to localize exactly where behavior diverges). Applied to 2,500 SWE-Bench Verified trajectories: file-level choice is too coarse to separate success from failure (function-level selection is what matters), anti-patterns act as corpus-level difficulty signals, and even resolved runs differ substantially in efficiency.

**PortKit applicability:** PortKit currently tracks conversions as binary pass/fail against compile + schema validation (#1268/#1857) with no structured view into *how* a run reached that outcome. TraceProbe's anti-pattern detectors map directly onto concrete PortKit failure modes: "search loop" → repeated non-converging Bedrock RAG lookups (#1189); "verification skip" → accepting a translated segment without running the compile-dispatch repair loop (#1857). The function-level-over-file-level finding also validates the segment-level granularity direction already taken in #1090/#1091. Converge-style trajectory alignment gives a real regression-testing tool for safely rolling out #1856 (milestone pipeline) and #1866 (Multisage) — comparing *how* translation happens, not just pass rate. Strong synergy with #1267 (execution diagnostic suite) and #1577 (eval pipeline audit).

### [2607.09510] Failure as a Process: An Anatomy of CLI Coding Agent Trajectories
- **arXiv:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.09510
- **Submitted:** July 10, 2026 · cs.SE
- **Relevance:** 72% — multi_agent_qa

**Summary:** First large-scale empirical study of CLI coding-agent failure trajectories as a *process* rather than a final outcome. Analyzed 1,794 execution trajectories (63,000+ steps) across seven frontier models and three scaffolds on Terminal-Bench. Key finding: failures are predominantly driven by epistemic errors, typically begin within the first few execution steps, and often remain hidden until recovery is no longer possible — meaning reliability requires earlier validation and intervention, not just final-outcome evaluation.

**PortKit horizon:** Reinforces the same direction as TraceProbe (#1876) and the milestone-decomposition work in #1856 from a different empirical angle: PortKit's repair loop (#1268/#1857) currently intervenes largely at compile/schema-check time, which is closer to "final outcome" than "early validation." This paper's finding that failures begin within the first few steps and stay hidden until unrecoverable is a case for instrumenting earlier checkpoints in the LangGraph pipeline (e.g., right after semantic parsing/chunking, before full translation) rather than only after a segment is fully translated. Not distinct enough from #1876/#1856 to warrant a separate issue this week — track as supporting evidence for prioritizing early-validation work in the repair loop.
